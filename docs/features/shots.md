# Shots, sets and locations

> **Built in step B6 ([#378](https://github.com/OpenClapper-dev/core/issues/378)).** Specification: [04 shots](../rewrite/04-shots.md), [09a places](../rewrite/09a-places.md), [I1 interjection](../rewrite/i1-interjection.md), [U3 shot](../rewrite/ux-3-shot.md). Replaces the first app's shots that owned photos, steps, layers and bakes, the review state, locations with spots and pins, and GPS on photos (review-and-tagging, composition, positions pages).

## The files (`packages/model/src/shot.ts`, `types.ts`)

| Type | What it is | References | Body |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `shot` | An intent: one planned picture of the film | `content` (any files, in order) | `{}` (version 3) |
| `progression` | Pictures of one shot over time, made only inside a shot (#426 feedback 4) | `item` (its pictures, in order), `overlay` (at most one) | `{ timing, frame, overlayShown }` |
| `set` | The place in the story where a scene plays (Motiv) | `content` | `{}` |
| `location` | The real place where it is filmed (Drehort) | `content` | `{}` |

- **Content** is an ordered list of references to files of any type of the project. Nothing is stored per entry; one file can be in any number of lists; a removed file leaves its entry, shown as missing.
- **A progression** is a timed order of pictures, a file of its own made inside a shot (`internal_of` the shot; the Worker refuses one anywhere else, and refuses to release it) and listed in the shot's content; a shot can hold several. An `item` reference points at a photo or a canvas (the Worker refuses another type with `400`; a missing one is kept) and carries `{ ms, frame?, move?, zoom? }`: its own time (100 ms to 10 min), for a canvas the frame it shows, for a smooth move the frame it moves to and whether it eases (`move: { to, ease }`), or for a **zoom block** the whole canvas run through its frames in their order (`zoom: { mode: "steps" | "smooth", ease }`: in steps each frame holds an equal part of `ms`; smooth from the first frame to the last; `itemAt`). The `overlay` reference is a canvas drawn over every item. The files a progression shows count as the shot's content without being listed.
- **Timing**: `each` (every item its own `ms`), `even` (one `total` split evenly) or `ends` (one `total`, the first and the last picture held twice as long); a zoom block in steps counts once per step; `itemTimes` computes it.
- **Default frame**: frame values (`FrameValues`: from a preset with a focal length, free-form, or an aspect ratio; the rules of a frame on a canvas). Applied, centred, to items with an angle of view and no frame of their own. A default frame from a preset keeps that lens in use: the Worker writes it into `frames` as frame `default` of the progression.
- **The lock**: a progression has no lock of its own; it is saved under its shot's lock (the Worker checks the shot's holder), and its save marks the shot changed and tells the shot's watchers to read again.
- **The thumbnail** (cover): the first entry of the content that is a photo, a canvas or a progression (a progression through its first item and its default frame; an empty one gives a placeholder), else a placeholder (`shotCover`).
- **Internal files**: a canvas made inside a shot (a new canvas made there, Draw or frame) is created with `internal_of` set to the shot; the generator's canvas and the overlay are created inside their progression. Internal files are hidden from the browser and search, binned, restored and copied with their parent (a copy of a shot copies its progressions and their canvases, each pointing at the copies), and released one way with `POST /api/files/:id/release` ([files](files.md)); a progression cannot be released.
- **Migration 0013** turned every shot's progression (its body and its `item`/`overlay` references) into a progression file inside it, named "Progression 1": listed first in the content when it had pictures (it was the cover), else last; the default frame's `frames` row and the internal canvases only the progression used moved along; every shot's body became `{}` at version 3.
- **Places properties are added where they are needed** (#426: a new project starts without properties): the map offers "Add a Position property" (on locations), a set's "Shots that play here" offers "Add a Set property" (on shots, one set), a location's "Sets filmed here" offers "Add a Locations property" (on sets, any number). Ordinary definitions: change, hide or delete them; any reference or position property of the project works the same.

## The Shot, Set and Location windows (`apps/studio/src/apps/ShotApp.tsx`, `src/shots/`)

One window per file, opened from any browser (double-click) or a chip. The default layout of U3: the **stage** with the chosen picture, **Properties** at the right, **Progression** (shots only) and **Content** below; panels are moved, sized, collapsed or stacked as tabs like everywhere (`Dock`), "Panels back to their places" resets them.

- **The stage** shows the picture at the progression's playhead (a caption names the progression and the time), or a content entry clicked to look at it ("Back to the playhead" returns); without a progression the first entry of the content. A picture is a photo, or a canvas (its render; one never saved shows its base photo with its frames), through its frame in Outline, Mask, Crop (default) or Full. Beyond the photo is hatched. The caption says which frame and where it came from (its own, the default frame, the canvas's picture). **Open** opens the file's own app; **Edit here** (a canvas, or a double-click) mounts the canvas editor in place of the stage and the side panel, with a bar naming the file and whether it is internal; **Draw or frame** on a photo makes an internal canvas on it, puts it after the photo and edits it here. An empty file offers Add files and Draw a canvas. A missing entry offers to restore the file (from the bin) or to remove the entry.
- **The side panel** has two tabs: the file of the window (thumbnail with the rule that chose it, name, the project's properties, for a set the shots that point at it, for a location the sets (with no reference property for that yet, a button adds "Set" or "Locations"), its internal files with what they are used for, Edit here, Make it a project file, Delete when unused, "Used in", System) and the chosen item or entry: one item of the progression (its kind, where it starts, its time, for a zoom block each step's time and its focal lengths, Ease and a slider to look through a smooth move, which frame of a canvas it shows, where its frame comes from, whether its file is listed or counts through the progression; then the file's own facts and properties), several chosen items (how long together, Delete them), or the content entry looked at.
- **Content** (changed by #426 feedback 4): cards or rows, reordered by dragging; a card shows its picture (a progression its first picture, with its number of items and length), its type, and which progressions use it. The first photo, canvas or progression is the shot's **thumbnail**: a "Thumbnail" badge marks it, and "Use as thumbnail" (in a card's menu, or the progression panel's head) moves an entry to the top. "Add files" opens a picker over the project (search, a type filter; a collection opens and its photos are ticked one by one or all; nothing internal); "Progression" makes a new progression and "New canvas" an internal canvas. A card's menu: Use as thumbnail, Edit here, Open, Draw or frame, Insert into the progression at the playhead (or start one), Make it a project file, Remove (an internal file is deleted with its entry once nothing of the shot points at it); a progression's: Use as thumbnail, Open in the progression panel, Rename, Duplicate, Delete. The same menu opens with a right click. A photo or canvas card dragged onto the progression's track becomes an item where it is dropped (onto the empty panel: a new progression). The files the progressions show that the list does not hold are listed below it ("count as content through a progression"); a right click lists one in the content or makes it the thumbnail.
- **Progression** (shots; changed by #426 feedback 4): one of the shot's progressions as a small timeline (`src/shots/ProgressionEditor.tsx`). The panel's head: a picker of the shot's progressions (switch, New progression, Rename, Duplicate, Delete; deleting takes its zooms and overlay along), its number of items and length, "Thumbnail of the shot" or "Use as thumbnail", Undo and Redo, **Fit** and a zoom slider (or Ctrl + wheel). A bar: **Play/Pause** (Space) runs it on the clock from the playhead and the stage follows, the time, **Timing** (each its own time, one length split evenly, or one length with the first and last picture held twice as long), the **default frame** (none, a preset with a focal length, free-form values, or an aspect ratio with its size), the **overlay** (a switch, Edit overlay; "Add an overlay" makes an internal canvas of the progression on transparent paper, edited here, stretched to each picture's frame), **Generate a zoom…** and **Add pictures…** (photos, canvases, or one frame of a canvas), both inserted at the playhead. The track: a ruler (click or drag to seek; the playhead's knob scrubs; ← → 0.1 s, Shift 1 s, Home, End), one lane of clips, each as wide as it holds with thumbnails (a zoom block one cell per step, a smooth move its two ends with an arrow) and its name, kind and time. Click chooses, Shift adds a range, Ctrl one more, a box drawn on the empty lane chooses what it touches, Ctrl+A all; dragging chosen clips moves them together (a line shows where); pulling a clip's right edge changes its time in tenths of a second (a zoom block in steps per step; in a split timing it switches to each its own time). Delete removes the chosen items (the files stay), Ctrl+X/C/V cut, copy and paste (after the chosen items or at the playhead; across progressions and shots), Ctrl+D duplicates, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z undo and redo (one history per progression while the window is open). A right click on a clip: Cut, Copy, Paste after, Duplicate, Delete, Edit the canvas here, Use this file as the thumbnail; on the empty lane: Paste here, Choose all, Generate a zoom here…, Add pictures here…. Photos and canvases dropped from a Files window or the desktop become items where they land. The keys are the "shot" scope ([shortcuts](../shortcuts.md)), off while the shot is mounted in a timeline. The ruler and playhead are the design system's `TimeRuler` and `Playhead`, which the Timeline and the master timeline use too.
- **The generator** makes zooms only, from a photo with an angle of view and a preset or free-form values (format, multiplier, squeeze, delivery). In steps: the ticked primes of a preset, or for a zoom preset and free values common stops, every so many millimetres, or a typed list (whole mm), between From and To (their order is the direction); each picture holds a time. Smooth: from one focal length to another over a time, eased or not; one moving picture. At most 60 frames. The preview shows every frame on the photo and says which see more than it. The result is one internal canvas with a frame per focal length on the photo (named "Framing series" or "Zoom", or as typed) inside the progression, and one zoom block that shows it, inserted at the playhead (or where the lane's menu was opened); without a progression it starts one.
- **The edit lock** (shots; sets and locations have none): one window edits a shot, the one that opened it first; others, the Shot window mounted in a timeline included, see it read-only (what changes it muted) with who edits it, Edit when it is free, and Stop editing for the editor. Watchers follow every change at once in a project set to realtime, else read it again every 5 s. Property values are not under the lock. On the phone a content change to a shot someone edits is refused with a message.
- **Saving**: every change shows at once and is saved 0.4 s later as one `PUT /api/files/:id` of the references (and a shot's body); the Studio's file store reads the project again after. The store holds internal files too (`GET /api/files?internal=1`); every list leaves them out.
- **Lists**: a shot's, set's or location's card in a browser shows its cover through its frame; rows show the cover's own thumbnail. A canvas never saved in the editor shows its base photo.
- **Transparent paper**: a blank canvas can have no colour (`surface.color: null`, "Transparent" in the surface's row; a checkerboard in the editor); its render is a PNG with transparency. Overlays start that way.

## Places: Sets, Locations and the map (`apps/studio/src/places/`)

- **Sets** and **Locations** are entries on the file browser, as Shots is: the browser set to that type, flat, with its properties as columns, "New set" / "New location" (named by the pattern), filters in the sidebar, the board. Files makes them too (New, Set / Location). A double-click opens the Set or Location window (above).
- **The map view** is a fourth view of the browser, next to list, grid and board, offered where it can show something: in Locations always (its first view), elsewhere once a listed file has a position. One pin per file with a position (the value of its first position property), in its type's colour; a click makes it current, its popup opens it; the view fits the pins once, and a note says how many have none. In a project without a position property the note offers "Add a Position property" instead (on locations). Leaflet 1.9.4 with OpenStreetMap's tiles (attribution, and a Referer of the origin as the tile server asks); in the dark theme the tiles are darkened. Nothing is looked up: an address is never sent anywhere.
- **The pin editor**: a position property has a pin button beside its text field (which takes "lat, lng" or a map link): a map where a click places the pin and a drag moves it; Keep saves it, "Remove the pin" clears it.
- **References open files**: a reference value's chip opens the file (its app's window); its cross takes it out of the value.

## On the phone and tablet (`apps/mobile/src/apps/Places.tsx`, `src/places/`)

- **Shots, Sets and Locations** are apps with a touch form, so each can be a tab (Setup, Tabs on this device; a new device has Shots). Each lists its files as tiles with their cover, cropped to its frame like the Studio's Crop; the plus makes a new one (named by the pattern; needs a connection). Locations opens on a **map** (Leaflet 1.9.4 and OpenStreetMap's tiles in a web view, as the first app did; needs a connection) with a pin per location with a position and a note of how many have none (without a position property it says to add one in the Studio, whose map offers it; the phone does not make property definitions); a tap opens it. List shows them as tiles.
- **The touch form**, one for all three: the chosen picture (cropped to its frame, the progression's default frame for photos, the overlay over a shot's pictures); for a shot with a progression, its thumbnail progression (or the first; a tap on another progression of the content plays that one) with its name, **Play** (on the clock; a smooth move plays as the frame between its two frames, a zoom block runs through its frames) and its pictures as a strip; the name (saved when the field is left); the **content** as a strip of pictures (tap to show; press and hold: Draw on it for a canvas, Earlier, Later, Remove from the content; a progression cannot be removed on the phone), **Add files** (photos, canvases, shots, sets, locations of the project) and **Draw a canvas** (a new internal canvas in the content, opened in the editor); for a set the shots that play there and for a location the sets filmed there (a tap opens them in their tab); then the properties, the same controls as for a photo in Media. Changes to the content and the name need a connection; property values wait on the phone until they can be sent, as for photos.
- **What the phone loads**: with Media's library, the shots, sets and locations of the project with their references, bodies and values, the progressions inside shots, and the canvases made inside shots and progressions (`internal=1`); a canvas's frames are read from the server when one of its pictures is shown.
- The progression is played, not edited, on the phone (ux-5); the Studio edits it.
