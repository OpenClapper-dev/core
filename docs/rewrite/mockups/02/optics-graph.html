How to read it

Top row, the photo. At import (or at capture in the app) one number is read into the photo's capture facts: the 35 mm-equivalent focal length. With the image's own width and height that gives the photo's angle of view by one formula, on demand. The raw EXIF stays on the photo, so nothing is lost by keeping only one number next to it.

Middle row, the camera you want to see. Optics is five plain factors. They can be typed in, or filled from a saved combination. No equipment, no lens sets, no vignetting.

Frames. Two angles give an optics frame. A ratio gives an aspect frame on any photo, with or without capture facts. A framing is either of them saved on a photo with a name and a centre. A framing made from a combination follows that combination: editing it changes those framings, after a notice that says how many.

Not in the graph: the phone's viewfinder (workshop 03). It needs the live picture's angle of view from the phone itself, then uses the same optics and the same ratio.

A photo without capture facts can only get aspect frames, until the "typed in by hand" way exists. A known limit: a photo that was cropped in another program usually keeps its EXIF, so its capture facts describe the uncropped picture and optics frames on it come out wrong without any warning.

Points to decide

  1. Capture facts as one number (35 mm-equivalent focal length), or focal length and sensor size kept separately?
  2. Typing the number in by hand: part of the first version, or later?
  3. The saved combination: what is it called, and does it belong to a project or to the workspace?
  4. Does a photo have a default framing ("root"), or does each user of the photo pick its own?
